Use @Video1 as the source footage for a cinematic character replacement. Recreate its reveal while replacing the original male with the person in @Image1 and the original female with the person in @Image2.

@Image1 defines the replacement male's face, complexion, build, hair, accessories, and outfit. If it contains multiple panels, they all show ONE person from different angles; do not generate a collage or extra people.
@Image2 defines the replacement female's face, complexion, build, hair, accessories, and outfit.
@Video1 defines the action, camera movement, shot sequence, setting, lighting, timing, and existing audio.

Keep the two identities separate. Replace each source person consistently across every shot in which they appear. Preserve the exact reference outfits. Do not merge faces, exchange roles, duplicate people, or retain the source faces.

Follow the source reveal as closely as possible: same starting poses, head angles, gaze directions, movement paths, speed, pauses, expressions, and ending poses. Reproduce the male's head lift wherever it occurs. Let the female follow her corresponding source actions. Do not invent gestures or emotional reactions.

This is a reveal, NOT a singing scene. Neither character sings, talks, or lip-syncs. Keep mouths naturally at rest with subtle breathing and the source's non-speaking facial movement.

Preserve the original shot order, cut points, camera angles, camera movement, focus changes, composition, transitions, and background. Keep each replacement in their source person's screen position and shots. Do not add a shared shot, extra cuts, new characters, new locations, or choreography.

Match each replacement naturally to the source lighting, shadows, perspective, exposure, haze, and depth of field. Keep faces, hair, hats, clothing, and jewelry stable throughout.

Match the source aspect ratio and duration where the selected output settings allow. Do not stretch the image or retime the action. If the output must be longer than the source, complete the reveal at the original pace, then gently continue the final composition for the remaining time. Do not loop or slow the whole sequence.

Use the source video's embedded audio as the reference if supported. No separate audio attachment is required for this reveal. Do not add singing, dialogue, new music, or sound effects.

Photorealistic people, natural anatomy and fabric. No face drift, morphing, source-face remnants, duplicate limbs, wardrobe flicker, captions, subtitles, logos, or split-screen panels.

Before you create.

Can I use any YouTube music video?

No. A public link is not permission to reuse a video, song, performance, or likeness. Use material you own or have suitable permission to adapt and publish. A remix tool does not clear those rights for you. The Chris Brown example identifies our visual reference; it is not a blanket license or an affiliation.

Will this copy every movement exactly?

No. It is a reference-guided generation, not a guaranteed frame-accurate replacement. Inspect faces, role assignments, shot order, transitions, and timing. A clean, short reference is a better first test than a complicated action scene.

Do I need Suno, H3, and Higgsfield?
